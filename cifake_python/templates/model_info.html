CIFAKE Architecture & Theoretical Foundations

Comprehensive guide to dataset composition, dual CNN models, and Explainable AI

What is CIFAKE?

CIFAKE is a benchmark computer vision dataset designed specifically to research synthetic image detection. It comprises 120,000 images (32×32 pixels, 3 color channels):

  • REAL (60,000 images): Authentic natural photographs sourced directly from CIFAR-10 across 10 categories (airplanes, automobiles, birds, cats, deer, dogs, frogs, horses, ships, trucks).
  • FAKE (60,000 images): Synthetically generated images generated using Latent Diffusion Models (Stable Diffusion 1.4) prompted to emulate CIFAR-10 classes.
What is Standard CNN2D?

A baseline 2D Convolutional Neural Network consisting of hierarchical convolutional layers, non-linear activation functions (ReLU), and max-pooling, followed by a traditional Flatten() operation that vectorizes spatial feature maps into a 1D tensor connected to dense layers.

Limitation: The Flatten step introduces hundreds of thousands of parameters in the dense layer, making it prone to memorizing specific synthetic noise patterns rather than generalizable semantic artifacts.

What is Modified CNN2D?

An enhanced architecture engineered to solve the shortcomings of the baseline CNN2D. It introduces two fundamental architectural improvements:

  1. Global Average Pooling (GAP): A structural regularizer that averages each feature map over its entire spatial domain.
  2. Dropout Regularization: Stochastic deactivation of neurons during training to prevent co-adaptation of synthetic feature detectors.
What is Global Average Pooling (GAP)?

Instead of flattening an 8×8×128 feature map into an 8,192-dimensional vector (requiring over 1,000,000 weights to connect to a dense layer), GAP computes the spatial mean of each slice, reducing 8×8×128 directly to 128 scalar values. There are no trainable weights in GAP, drastically preventing overfitting.

What is Dropout?

Dropout randomly sets a fraction (e.g. 25% to 40%) of input units to 0 at each update during training. This forces individual convolutional filters and projection neurons to learn independent, robust representations of AI diffusion artifacts rather than relying on brittle correlations.

What is Grad-CAM & Why is it Critical?

Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) computes the gradients of the classification score with respect to the final convolutional feature maps. It produces a coarse 2D localization heatmap highlighting the visual regions that exerted the highest influence on the classification output.

Scientific Value: In synthetic image detection, Grad-CAM allows human reviewers to verify whether the model is focusing on genuine diffusion anomalies (e.g., distorted eye pupils, blurred backgrounds, unnatural edges) or cheating on irrelevant border pixels.
